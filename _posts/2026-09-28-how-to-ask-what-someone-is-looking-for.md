There is one question almost everyone on a dating app wants answered, and almost
nobody asks directly: what are you actually looking for?

Among Indian and NRI singles the range of possible answers is unusually wide.
Some people want to be married within two years, with their families involved
from the start. Some want something serious without a timeline. Some have
recently moved countries and mostly want company. A few want something casual
and are hoping not to have to say so. And occasionally someone is not as single
as their profile suggests.

None of these is wrong. But finding out which one you are talking to, early, is
the single biggest time saver in dating.

## Why people avoid asking

Usually it is fear of seeming intense. Nobody wants to be the person who brings
up marriage in the second message, and there is a widespread belief that showing
you want something serious will scare people off.

There is also hope. If you like someone, it is tempting not to ask a question
whose answer might end things.

The result is that two people can spend months together on different tracks,
each assuming the other is on theirs. That costs far more than one slightly
awkward conversation.

## What the common answers usually mean

People tend to answer with stock phrases. It helps to know roughly what each one
signals, while remembering that the only way to be sure is a follow-up question.

**"Something serious" or "long term."** A good sign, but broad. It can mean
marriage within a year or a committed relationship with no plans beyond it.

**"Marriage, eventually."** Worth one more question about what eventually means.
For one person it is two years; for another it is a vague point in their late
thirties.

**"Open to anything" or "seeing where it goes."** Sometimes genuinely open.
Often it means they have not thought about it, or they want something more
casual than they expect you do.

**"Friendship first."** Can be completely sincere, particularly from people who
have recently moved and are rebuilding a social life. Can also be a softer way of
saying they are not ready for anything else.

**"Nothing too serious right now."** Believe it. This is one of the few answers
that means exactly what it says.

And one to handle with care: if someone mentions a spouse, or their profile says
they are married, and they describe what they want as "just friends", take them
at their word about the marriage and decide whether that is something you want
any part of.

## When to ask

Not in the opening message. A first message about marriage plans reads like a job
application.

The natural moment is once there is some real rapport: a few days into a good
conversation, or during a first call. If marriage is what you are looking for, it
is entirely reasonable to have raised it before you meet in person. You are not
asking for a commitment. You are checking that you are both playing the same
game.

## How to ask without it feeling like an interview

### Go first

The easiest way to get an honest answer is to give one. "I'm on here because I'd
like to meet someone I could build a life with. What about you?" is much easier
to answer than a cold "so what are you looking for?"

It also takes the pressure off the other person. You have already said the
serious thing, so they do not have to.

### Ask about direction, not deadlines

"I'm looking for something that leads to marriage" is an open, honest statement.
"I want to be married within a year" can sound like a test with a timer on it,
even when you mean it gently.

Direction tells people what you want. Deadlines tell them how much pressure you
are under. Lead with the first.

### Make honesty easy

Say out loud that there is no wrong answer: "Honestly, any answer is fine. I'd
just rather know." People are far more likely to admit they want something
casual when they are not braced for disappointment.

### Follow-up questions that tell you more

A few questions reveal more than "what are you looking for" ever will:

- "What would you like your life to look like in a couple of years?"
- "Where do you see yourself living long term?"
- "How involved is your family in all this?"
- "What made you join an app now?"

That last one is particularly useful. "I've just moved and want to meet people"
and "my parents have started sending me biodatas" are very different answers.

## Listen for the answer under the answer

What someone says is useful. What they do is more useful.

People looking for something serious tend to make time, make plans, and are
curious about your life beyond the next date. People looking for something
lighter tend to keep things vague, keep plans last minute, and steer
conversations back to the present.

Watch for consistency. Someone who says they want marriage but avoids any
conversation about the future, or who after several weeks says they are "not into
labels", has answered the question, just not in words. Our guide to
[green flags]({{ '/green-flags-what-a-healthy-relationship-looks-like/' | relative_url }})
covers what steady, serious interest tends to look like.

## The family question

For many NRI singles, "what are you looking for" has a second layer: how
involved is your family?

It helps to know whether someone's parents know they are dating, whether family
introductions are happening in parallel, and how much weight their family's views
on community or religion will carry. None of these is a dealbreaker on its own,
but they shape everything later.

Ask gently and without judgement. Our posts on the
[middle ground between arranged marriage and dating]({{ '/arranged-marriage-vs-dating-nri-middle-ground/' | relative_url }})
and on [talking to Indian parents about dating]({{ '/talking-to-indian-parents-about-dating/' | relative_url }})
go further into how these two worlds overlap.

## When you want different things

This is the part people skip. If someone tells you clearly that they want
something different from you, believe them.

Hoping someone will change their mind about marriage, children or which country
to live in is the most common way people lose a year. Sometimes it happens. Far
more often, both people spend months quietly waiting for the other to move.

A kind exit is short: "I've really enjoyed talking to you, but I think we're
looking for different things, and I'd rather be honest about that now." Nobody
enjoys sending that message. Almost everyone respects receiving it.

If you are genuinely unsure, agree a check-in point instead: "Let's see how we
feel in a couple of months and talk about it properly then." Then actually have
that conversation.

## Say it on your profile too

The easiest way to avoid mismatches is to filter for them before the first
message. Saying clearly on your profile what you are looking for means the people
who message you have already read it.

## The short version

Ask what someone is looking for once there is some rapport, and before things get
serious. Go first, describe a direction rather than a deadline, and make it clear
that any honest answer is welcome. Then watch whether what they do matches what
they said.

If your answers do not line up, believe that early. The right person will want
the same thing you do, and the sooner you are free to find them, the better.
