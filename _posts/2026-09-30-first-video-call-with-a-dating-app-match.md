There is a step between chatting and meeting that a lot of people skip, and it is
the most useful one: a short video call.

Twenty minutes on a call tells you more than two weeks of messages. You find out
whether the person matches their photos, whether the conversation flows when
neither of you can edit your replies, and whether you actually want to spend an
evening with them. For couples in different cities or countries, it is often the
whole relationship for a while.

It is also the moment many people dread. It does not need to be.

## Why it is worth doing

**It confirms the person is real.** A live call is the single most reliable way
to rule out a fake profile. Photos can be borrowed. A face responding to you in
real time cannot.

**It shows you the chemistry.** Some people who write brilliantly are flat in
conversation. Others who send one-line replies turn out to be warm and funny on a
call. You cannot tell which from text.

**It saves time and money.** A call that does not click costs twenty minutes. A
first date that does not click costs an evening, the travel and the dinner.

## Suggesting it

### When

Once you have had a few days of good conversation and you are thinking about
meeting, suggest a call first. Earlier is fine too. The longer you leave it, the
more of an idea of someone you build up, and the bigger the gap can feel if they
turn out to be different.

### How

Keep it light and specific:

"I'm enjoying this. Fancy a quick video call this week? Twenty minutes, no
pressure. Thursday or Saturday evening?"

Offering a short call with two concrete times makes it easy to say yes. If you
are in different time zones, which is common for NRI matches, offer a time that
works at both ends rather than asking one person to call at midnight.

### Use a link, not a number

You do not need to swap phone numbers to video call. A Google Meet or Zoom link
works on any phone and keeps your number private until you have decided you want
to share it. For a first call that is a sensible default, and nobody reasonable
will mind. If a match is pushing hard for your number before any call at all,
our post on
[matches who want to move to WhatsApp straight away]({{ '/when-a-match-wants-to-move-to-whatsapp/' | relative_url }})
is worth a read.

## Setting it up without overthinking it

A few small things make a noticeable difference:

- **Light in front of you, not behind you.** Sitting with a window at your back
  turns you into a silhouette. Face the window or a lamp instead.
- **Camera at eye level.** Prop your phone on a few books rather than holding it
  below your chin.
- **Somewhere quiet.** Not a busy café, not in a car, and ideally not from bed.
- **Headphones if you have them.** They cut out echo and make it easier to hear
  each other properly.
- **A drink in hand.** Chai or coffee gives your hands something to do and makes
  it feel more like a meeting than an interview.

Dress as you would for a relaxed first date. You are not presenting. You are
having a conversation.

## What to talk about

The good news is that you are not starting from nothing. Pick up the threads from
your messages: the trip they mentioned, the dish from their home state they were
defending, the work deadline they were nervous about.

A few topics work well on almost any first call:

- **Their city.** What brought them there, what they like, what they miss.
- **Home.** Where they grew up, what their family is like, how often they go back.
- **Food.** Endlessly easy, especially across regional cuisines.
- **Weekends.** What they actually do with their time tells you a lot.
- **Plans.** What they are looking forward to, at work or outside it.

Ask follow-up questions, and answer with a little more than you were asked. A
call where both people are curious almost always goes well.

Some things are better left for later: the full story of your last relationship,
salaries, and detailed marriage timelines in the first five minutes. It is fine
for what you are each looking for to come up, and our post on
[how to ask what someone is looking for]({{ '/how-to-ask-what-someone-is-looking-for/' | relative_url }})
covers how to do that naturally. Just do not make it the agenda.

Silences are normal. They feel longer on a screen than they are.

## What to notice

### Good signs

They look like their photos. They ask you questions as well as answering yours.
The person on the call is recognisably the person from the messages. And at some
point you notice that you have stopped thinking about the call itself.

### Warning signs

Most first calls are uneventful. A few things are worth noticing, though.

- **The camera never works.** Once is a bad connection. Every time is a pattern.
- **You never quite see them.** The face is always half in shadow, the video
  freezes at convenient moments, or it looks oddly smooth and does not react to
  what you say. Recorded clips and heavy filters exist, and they cannot answer a
  spontaneous question.
- **The details do not match.** They say it is evening in Toronto, and there is
  bright daylight behind them. Small inconsistencies like this deserve a gentle
  question.
- **Pressure towards anything intimate.** End the call. Screens can be recorded,
  and a request like this on a first call is a known route into blackmail.

None of this needs to be confrontational. If something feels off, you can simply
end the call politely and think about it afterwards.

## Ending the call well

End on a high rather than letting it fade. If it went well, say so and suggest
the next step while you are both enjoying it: "This was really nice. Would you
like to meet for coffee next weekend?"

If it did not click, you do not need to decide on the spot. A message afterwards
is kinder and easier than a verdict on camera: "It was lovely talking to you, but
I didn't feel quite the connection I was hoping for. I wish you the best." Our
guide to
[handling rejection on dating apps]({{ '/handling-rejection-and-ghosting-on-dating-apps/' | relative_url }})
covers the other side of that message.

## When the call is the relationship for now

For couples in different countries, video calls are not a step on the way to
meeting. For a while, they are the relationship.

That changes how you use them. Shorter, more frequent calls tend to work better
than marathon sessions at the weekend. Doing something together on the call, such
as cooking the same dish, watching the same film or walking through your
neighbourhood, gives you shared memories rather than another update. Our guide to
[long-distance relationships for NRI couples]({{ '/long-distance-relationship-tips-for-nri-couples/' | relative_url }})
goes into this in more detail.

## A few safety basics

- Glance at your background before the call. A house number, a work badge or the
  view from your window can say more than you mean to.
- Keep your address, workplace and financial details back until you have met.
- Assume anything shown on camera could be recorded.
- Keep chatting on the dating app until you have met in person. If something goes
  wrong, that conversation is how you report the account.

## The short version

Suggest a short video call before you meet, with two concrete times and a link
rather than a phone number. Face the light, keep it to about twenty minutes, and
talk about the things you were already talking about. Notice whether they are the
person in their photos. Then end on a high and suggest the next step.

It is the cheapest, most useful twenty minutes in dating.
