You match with someone, trade three or four messages, and then it arrives: "I'm
not very active on here. Add me on WhatsApp?" with a number underneath.

Most of the time this is harmless. Plenty of people live on WhatsApp and treat
dating apps as somewhere to find people rather than somewhere to talk to them.
But the same message is also the opening move of almost every romance scam, and
from the outside the two look identical.

You do not need to treat every request as a threat. You just need a way of
responding that works well either way, and a sense of which details separate an
eager person from a script.

## Why the request is so common

### The ordinary reasons

There are plenty of innocent explanations. People check WhatsApp fifty times a
day and a dating app twice. Voice notes and photos are easier there. Some people
feel a conversation is not quite real until it sits in the same place as their
friends and family. And some simply want to move things along because they like
you.

None of that is a problem. The question is not whether someone asks, but how and
when.

### Why scam accounts want it even more

For someone running a scam, getting you off the app is the first objective,
because the app is the only place they can be stopped:

- **On the app, they can be reported and removed.** Off it, a block only stops
  them contacting you, and they keep working through everyone else.
- **On the app, the conversation is on record.** Off it, there is nothing for a
  platform to review.
- **Your number opens doors.** A phone number is often linked to your name, your
  WhatsApp photo, your social accounts and, in India especially, your payment
  apps. It also lets them reach you after you have blocked them on the app.
- **It is efficient.** Scam accounts run many conversations at once, and moving
  everyone to one messaging app makes that easier to manage.

That is why the request usually comes so early. The longer you stay on the app,
the more chances you have to notice something and report it.

## What the script tends to sound like

Scam conversations are remarkably similar to each other, because the same
scripts get passed around. A few patterns come up again and again.

**"I'm not really active here."** Usually within the first five messages, often
with a number attached before you have learned much about them at all.

**A reason the app does not work for them.** Their subscription is about to run
out, they have used up their free messages, the app keeps crashing, they are
about to delete their account. There is always a reason the conversation has to
move today.

**A preference for apps that are not tied to an identity.** A Telegram username,
Signal, or a lesser-known app such as Zangi, where an account can exist without
revealing who is behind it. Insisting on one of these rather than an ordinary
call is worth noticing.

**Flattery and urgency together.** "You are so beautiful, I feel a real
connection, let's talk properly over there." Warmth that arrives this quickly is
doing a job.

**Details that do not quite hold together.** A number from a different city or
country than the one they say they live in. A second number a week later because
"that was my old one". On its own, a mismatched number means little, since
people keep their numbers when they move. Combined with the other signals, it
adds up.

**Pressure when you say not yet.** Guilt ("I thought you were serious"),
sulking, or simply vanishing the moment it is clear you will not move.

Any one of these can be innocent. The combination, especially at speed, is the
pattern.

## How genuine people tend to ask

The contrast is useful. When someone who is simply keen suggests moving to
WhatsApp, it usually looks like this:

- It comes after a real conversation, not instead of one.
- The reason is about the two of you ("it would be easier to send voice notes"),
  not about why they cannot be on the app.
- They are happy to do a video call first, and often suggest it themselves.
- "Not yet" gets an easy "no problem", and the conversation carries on.

## What to say back

You do not need to accuse anyone of anything. A friendly, clear reply works for
both kinds of person, because a genuine match will accept it and a scam account
will not.

**If you are not ready yet:** "I like to chat here a bit longer before swapping
numbers. Happy to do a quick video call this week though."

**If you are open to it, after a check:** "Sure, let's do a quick video call
first. Does Thursday evening work for you?"

**If they have already sent their number:** You are not obliged to use it.
"Thanks! I'll stick to chatting here for now" is a complete answer.

Then watch what happens. Someone who replies "of course, Thursday works" has
passed the only test that matters at this stage. Someone who pushes, sulks or
disappears has saved you a great deal of time.

## If you do move, move carefully

Moving to WhatsApp after a good conversation and a video call is completely
normal. A few habits make it safer:

- **Do the first video call by link.** A Google Meet or Zoom link lets you see
  each other without either of you sharing a number. Our guide to the
  [first video call with a match]({{ '/first-video-call-with-a-dating-app-match/' | relative_url }})
  covers how to make it easy.
- **Check your WhatsApp privacy settings.** You can limit who sees your profile
  photo, your "about" line and when you were last online. Setting these to your
  contacts only is a sensible default.
- **Consider a second number for dating.** Some people keep a separate number for
  exactly this, and give out their main one later.
- **Do not unmatch on the app straight away.** If something goes wrong after you
  move, the original conversation is how you report the account.
- **Keep identifying details back for now.** Your workplace, your address and
  anything financial can wait until you have met in person.

## When to walk away and report

Some requests end the question entirely, however long you have been talking:

- Any request for money, for any reason, from someone you have not met
- Any conversation that turns towards investing, crypto or "trading platforms"
- Any request for intimate photos, or pressure after you have said no
- Any threat, of any kind

Report the account on the dating app even if you have already moved to WhatsApp
and blocked them there. The account is still on the app, having the same
conversation with someone else. Our
[online dating safety guide]({{ '/online-dating-safety-tips-for-indian-singles/' | relative_url }})
covers what to do if you have already sent money or are being threatened.

## The short version

An early request to move to WhatsApp is not a red flag on its own. Speed, a
story about why they cannot stay on the app, an unusual messaging app and
pressure when you say not yet are. Reply warmly, suggest a video call by link
first, and let their response tell you who you are talking to.

Most people will pass that test without noticing it was one. The few who do not
were never going to be worth the number.
