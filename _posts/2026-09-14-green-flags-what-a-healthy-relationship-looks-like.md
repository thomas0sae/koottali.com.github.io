There is no shortage of advice about what to avoid. Red flags, warning signs,
things he does that mean he is not serious - the internet is well supplied.

What almost nobody writes about is the other half: what a good relationship
actually looks like from the inside. And because it looks fairly ordinary, a lot
of people who are in one are not sure whether they are, while a lot of people in
a bad one are certain it is love because it is intense.

Here is what good tends to look like.

## It is consistent

The single most reliable sign is boring: what they say and what they do line up,
over and over, including when it is inconvenient.

They say they will call on Sunday and they call on Sunday. They say they are bad
at texting during the week, and then they are bad at texting during the week -
predictably, not selectively. Plans made are plans kept, or changed in advance
with a reason.

This sounds like a low bar and it is not. A great deal of relationship anxiety is
produced entirely by inconsistency: warmth on Tuesday, distance on Thursday, and
you spending Wednesday working out what you did. With a consistent person, that
whole category of worry simply does not appear.

If you find you have stopped checking your phone to see whether they have
replied, that is not indifference. That is what security feels like.

## You can disagree and it is fine

Couples who never argue are not usually more compatible. They are usually
avoiding something.

What you want is not an absence of conflict but a good version of it:

- You can say the thing that is bothering you without rehearsing it for two days
- They listen to the complaint rather than to the tone it was delivered in
- Nobody threatens the relationship itself during an argument
- It ends, and an hour later you are warm to each other again
- One of you apologises without needing to establish who was more wrong

**Repair matters more than the argument.** Every couple mishandles things. The
difference in the ones that last is how quickly they come back and how little
scorekeeping accumulates in between.

## They are interested in the rest of your life

A good sign, and an easy one to miss: they want you to have other people.

They ask about your friends by name. They are pleased when something goes well at
work. They do not compete with your family for your time, and they do not
interpret an evening spent elsewhere as something taken from them.

The opposite - being slowly narrowed down to one person - is one of the things
covered in our post on
[red flags worth taking seriously]({{ '/five-red-flags-to-watch-for-while-dating/' | relative_url }}).
The healthy version is that your world gets bigger, not smaller.

## You are more yourself, not less

Ask the question directly: who am I when I am around this person?

In a good relationship you talk more rather than less. You say the slightly odd
thing rather than editing it. You are not performing a calmer, funnier, more
agreeable version of yourself and quietly hoping they never meet the real one.

The inverse is subtle and common. People often describe a relationship as
exciting when what they mean is that they are anxious in it - attentive,
alert, managing. That feeling is easy to mistake for intensity, and it is not
the same thing at all.

## Comfortable silence

Early on, everything is conversation. The test comes later, when you have run out
of the obvious material.

Being able to sit in a car, or a room, for twenty minutes without talking, and
for that to be pleasant rather than something to be fixed - that is a real
signal. Most of a long relationship is made of exactly this: ordinary time spent
in the same place, not doing anything much.

If the silences are still work after six months, that is worth noticing.

## They are decent when nothing is at stake

How someone behaves when there is no benefit to behaving well tells you nearly
everything.

Watch how they speak to waiters, drivers and call-centre staff. Watch how they
talk about their exes when they are not being asked to be gracious. Watch what
happens when a plan collapses, a flight is delayed, or something is genuinely
their fault.

Kindness that appears only in front of an audience, or only towards people who
are useful, is not kindness. It is presentation, and it wears off.

## The practical things get discussed, not avoided

Healthy couples get to the awkward subjects reasonably early and without a
production being made of them: money, family expectations, where you would want
to live, whether you want children.

You do not need agreement on all of it in month three. You need to be able to
raise it and get a real answer rather than a deflection. A partner who changes
the subject every time the future comes up is telling you something, and it is
usually the thing you are worried it is.

## They like you, and it is not a puzzle

The last one is the simplest. You are not decoding anything.

They are pleased to see you and it shows. They say what they think about the
relationship rather than leaving you to infer it. You know where you stand, and
you did not have to ask twice.

An enormous amount of dating culture treats uncertainty as romantic - the chase,
the tension, not knowing. In practice, not knowing is just not knowing, and
relationships that start in ambiguity tend to stay there.

## What none of this looks like

Worth stating plainly, because green flags are quiet:

None of this is dramatic. There is no grand gesture in the list. Good
relationships are less cinematic than bad ones, which is exactly why people
sometimes leave them out of a suspicion that it should feel more like something.

Calm is not the absence of chemistry. For most people it is the condition under
which chemistry turns into a life.

## The short version

Look for consistency, repair after conflict, interest in your wider life, ease
in silence, decency when it costs something, willingness to discuss the
practical, and clarity about how they feel.

If most of those are present, you are probably in a good relationship even if it
does not feel like a film. If almost none of them are and it feels enormous,
that is worth sitting with.
