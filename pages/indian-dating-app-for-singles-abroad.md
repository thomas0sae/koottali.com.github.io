If you are Indian and living abroad, dating has a specific problem attached to
it, and it is not a shortage of apps.

The problem is that mainstream dating apps sort people by how physically close
they are. That works well in a city where almost everyone is a plausible match.
It works badly when the thing you are looking for — someone who shares your
language, understands what your family expects, and knows why you disappear for
three days at Onam — is spread thinly across an entire country.

NRI Meet is built for that situation. It is a **free Indian dating app for
singles abroad**, and it sorts on the things that actually narrow the field.

## Why a general dating app struggles for NRI singles

### Distance is the wrong first filter

On a mainstream app, a 25km radius in a mid-sized European or American city may
surface almost nobody from a similar background. Widen it and you get volume
without relevance.

What you usually want is the opposite shape: a wider geography, filtered harder
on background. Someone two hours away who speaks Malayalam and wants to be
married within a couple of years is a better prospect than two hundred people
within walking distance who do not.

### You end up explaining yourself

The recurring complaint from Indian and NRI singles on general apps is not
rudeness. It is the sheer amount of context-setting — explaining why your
parents need to be involved, why a wedding is a five-day event, why you fast on
certain days, why you cannot simply move cities on short notice.

None of that is difficult to explain once. It is exhausting to explain forty
times.

### Intentions are mismatched

A significant proportion of people on mainstream apps are not looking for
anything long-term, which is entirely reasonable and entirely useless if you
are. Being able to say plainly that you are looking for marriage — and to be
seen mainly by people who read that as normal rather than intense — removes
months of wasted conversation.

## How NRI Meet works

### Matching on what narrows the field

Every profile carries the information that actually predicts compatibility for
this community, and the app uses it directly:

- **Language** — Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, Hindi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Bengali,
  Kannada, Marathi and more
- **Home state and city in India**
- **Where you live now** — city, state and country
- **Interests**, education and profession
- **Age range and what you are looking for**

Shared language, the same home state, and the same current city all push a
profile higher in your recommendations. Fill the fields in and you are both
more interesting to read and genuinely seen by more people.

## Dating app, matrimony site, or both?

This is the question most people in the diaspora are actually weighing, and the
honest answer is that the two have been converging for years.

Matrimony sites are good at intent and at family involvement, and weak at
letting you get to know someone properly before a decision. Dating apps are good
at knowing someone first, and weak at direction — they can drift for years.

## Getting started well

Three things make most of the difference in the first fortnight:

1. **Use a clear solo first photo.** A group shot as your first image is the
   single most common reason a good profile gets scrolled past.
2. **Fill in every field, and say what you want.** If you are looking for
   marriage, write it. It is the most effective filter you have.
3. **Widen your distance filter before you conclude there is nobody.** Most
   people set it far too tight for the reality of a diaspora community.
